Four rules

  1. 01

    Put it where people start

    Byline, title page, first seconds of a video, top of a README. A badge buried at the end discloses nothing.

  2. 02

    Label the finished work

    Judge what was published, not every draft along the way. If parts differ a lot, label the parts separately.

  3. 03

    When unsure, go one tier up

    If you're torn between two tiers, choose the more automated one. Over-disclosing costs nothing; under-disclosing costs trust.

  4. 04

    Don't restyle the badge

    Keep the colours and codes as they are so people learn to recognise them. Use the chip by default and the circle only where a chip can't fit.

Questions

Is this a legal or official standard?

No. It's a voluntary convention that one person started and anyone can use. It isn't certified, audited or recognised by any regulator, and it doesn't replace any disclosure a law, publisher or platform requires of you.

Does anyone verify the badge?

No. It is a self-declaration, like a byline. The badge says what the maker reports, and their reputation stands behind it.

What counts as AI?

Generative and machine-learning systems that produce or judge content: chat assistants, image and audio generators, coding agents, AI grammar and review tools. Deterministic tools such as spellcheck, calculators, filters and compilers do not count.

I used AI only to brainstorm. Which tier?

If none of its output ended up in the work, {{T:HC}}. If its ideas or wording shaped what you published, {{T:M1}} or higher.

AI fixed my grammar and reworded a few sentences. Is that still HC?

Yes. {{T:HC}} covers checking and minor touch-ups that don't change the content: typos, grammar, simple rewording. Once AI writes new passages or changes what the work says, it is {{T:M1}}.

My project has parts with very different AI involvement.

Label the whole with the tier that best describes it overall, or label the parts separately: for example, text {{T:H}} and illustrations {{T:A2}}.

An AI picked my tier and added the badge. Does that change the tier?

No. The badge covers the content only. Using an AI to choose or insert the label is not counted.

Is a higher tier worse?

No. The scale describes how something was made, not how good it is. The point is that people can tell.

Can I use it commercially? Do I need permission?

Yes, and no permission needed. The HAI Scale License lets anyone use the scale for free, including on commercial work, with three conditions: label truthfully, keep the tiers and badges intact, and don't sell access to the scale itself. Charging for your own product that shows a badge is fine.

Will the tiers change?

This is version {{VERSION}}. The seven codes are meant to stay stable; wording may be refined. Changes will be versioned here.
